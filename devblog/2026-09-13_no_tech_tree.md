# Dev Blog: Why Stone Age Clans Has No Tech Tree

**Date:** September 13, 2026  
**Topic:** Production Tiers, Human Mastery, and Why Research Trees Miss the Point

---

## Research Trees Are a Lie

Let me start with something controversial: **research trees don't make sense for a Stone Age game.**

Most colony sims have you click "Research Bronze Working," wait for a timer, and boom — everyone in your tribe can now make bronze tools. Forever. As if knowledge alone is enough.

But that's not how human craft evolution worked. And it's definitely not how **mastery** worked.

The problem with research trees is they treat craftsmanship like a binary switch: either you **can't** make something, or you **can**. There's no middle ground. No apprenticeship. No years spent refining technique. No difference between the person who just learned to scrape a hide and the master tanner who's been doing it for 30 years.

**Stone Age Clans** is trying to simulate something more human: **the evolution of craft through infrastructure, time, and specialization.** Not because you clicked "Unlock Tunics," but because your community grew large and stable enough to support dedicated artisans.

---

## The Master Craftsperson Makes Everything Better

Here's the thing about real master craftspeople: **they don't just make "new" things.** They make *everything* better.

A master tailor doesn't wake up one day and suddenly unlock the ability to sew robes. They've been making cloaks for years — but their cloaks are **so much better** than a novice's that it's almost a different item.

The stitching is tighter. The hide is cut to fit the body instead of just draped. The dye work is flawless. The cloak doesn't just keep you warm — it sheds rain, moves with you when you run, and maybe even makes you look intimidating.

**That's what our tier system simulates.**

A **Tier 1 tailor** makes a **Common cloak** — functional, fast, good enough to survive.

A **Tier 3 tailor** makes a **Master cloak** — still a cloak, but crafted with such refined technique that it's 50% more effective. Same item. Different hands.

And here's the kicker: **the Tier 3 tailor can't turn it off.** They don't have a "make bad cloaks" button. Their baseline is mastery. Even when they're making the "basic" item, it comes out better because **they are better.**

That's human. That's realistic. And that's what research trees completely miss.

---

## The Flint Sword That Mocked Bronze

There's a real historical example of this I love, and it perfectly captures what we're going for.

Archaeologists have found **flint swords** from the late Stone Age that are shaped **exactly like early bronze swords.** They're not functional — you can't fight with them; they'd shatter. They're ceremonial. Status symbols.

But here's what makes them incredible: **they required insane skill to make.**

Bronze is easy to cast into a sword shape. You heat it, pour it, let it cool. A bronze sword is impressive because **bronze is new and rare.**

But flint? Flint doesn't melt. You have to knap it — chip away at a stone, flake by flake, with perfect pressure and angle, until you've carved a blade shape that mimics the smooth curves of cast bronze. **One mistake and the whole thing shatters.**

These flint swords weren't trying to be functional. They were a flex. The message was clear:

> *"You think your fancy new Bronze Age tech is impressive? My stone knapping is so good I can copy your garbage with a rock."*

That's **mastery**. That's **craft pride**. And that's the feeling we want in Stone Age Clans.

A Tier 3 artisan isn't just unlocking new recipes. They're **showing off**. Their "basic" items are so well-made they rival or surpass what lesser clans produce with "better" materials or "newer" tech.

## How Craft Actually Evolved in the Stone Age

Let's talk about how this worked in reality, because it's way more interesting than a tech tree.

### Phase 1: Everyone Does Everything (Survival Mode)

Small nomadic groups didn't have specialists. If you needed a hide scraped, you scraped it yourself. If you needed a spear, you made one. The quality varied wildly — some people were naturally better, but **nobody had time to perfect their craft** because everyone spent most of their day finding food.

**This is Tier 1 in our system:** one worker, part-time craft, Common quality output. Fast and functional, but nothing special.

### Phase 2: Surplus Creates Specialists (The Apprentice System)

When a group settled and started farming or had a really good hunting ground, something changed: **not everyone needed to hunt every day.**

For the first time, you could have two people focus on tanning hides while the rest of the group brought in food. One person taught the other. They experimented. They refined their process. The quality went up.

But they weren't artisans yet — if the food situation got bad, they'd drop their tools and go hunt. Specialization was conditional on surplus.

**This is Tier 2:** two workers, dedicated time, Good quality output (+25% over Common). The craft is getting refined, but it's still tied to the group's stability.

### Phase 3: Mastery as a Way of Life (Artisan Class)

In large, stable settlements, something remarkable happened: some people **never hunted.** They didn't gather. They didn't farm. They just... made things.

They had 10,000 hours of practice. They'd trained apprentices who'd gone on to train others. The techniques had been refined across generations. Their hands moved with muscle memory so deep they could probably tan a hide in their sleep.

At this point, the craft wasn't just better — it was **art**. A master tanner's leather wasn't just softer; it had patterns, dyes, tooling. A master flintknapper didn't just make spearheads; they made ceremonial blades that took weeks and were meant to be admired, not used.

**This is Tier 3:** three workers, never hunt, Master quality baseline (+50%), rare chance for Legendary. The craft has become culture.

## Why This Matters for Stone Age Clans

We're not making a game about clicking "Research Pottery" and calling it a day. We're making a game about **communities growing, specialists emerging, and craft becoming art over time.**

Here's how our tier system captures that:

### The Tier System

| Tier | Workers | Their Life | Output Quality |
|------|---------|------------|----------------|
| **Tier 1** | 1 worker | Part-time; hunts when idle | **Common** (base stats) |
| **Tier 2** | 2 workers | Rarely hunts; focus on craft | **Good** (+25% stats) |
| **Tier 3** | 3 workers | Never hunts; pure artisans | **Master** (+50% stats) |

### The Human Touch

What makes this feel **real**:

1. **Masters make everything better.** A Tier 3 tailor's "basic cloak" is Master quality. Their skill shows in everything they touch.

2. **Specialization has a cost.** You can't upgrade every building. You need population to sustain non-hunters. You're choosing what your clan becomes good at.

3. **Mastery is earned, not unlocked.** Getting to Tier 3 means your clan survived long enough, grew large enough, and stockpiled enough surplus to support full-time artisans. That's a story, not a button press.

4. **Artisans are vulnerable.** If your Tier 3 compound gets raided and your three artisans die, **you lose that mastery.** It's not a permanent unlock. It lives in those people.

5. **Other clans know.** When you see a three-hut Tier 3 compound in enemy territory, you know: this clan is thriving, their gear is better, and they're a valuable target.

## What This Means for Gameplay

### Early Game: Everyone Fights

You settle your first flag. You build a **Tier 1 Tailor**. The worker makes Common cloaks when you need them, but they also hunt, gather, and defend. Your clan is scrappy. Your gear is functional. You survive.

### Mid Game: Choosing Your Identity

Your clan grows to 10 people. You have enough food surplus to upgrade one building to **Tier 2**. Do you pick the Tailor (Good armor) or the Forge (Good weapons)?

You can't afford both. This choice defines your clan. Maybe you become the "heavy armor clan" — hard to kill but slower. Your rival picks weapons and becomes "fast raiders."

Now you **need** what they have. Do you trade? Do you raid? Do you invest in catching up (expensive, slow)?

**This is strategic depth.** Not "everyone researches everything," but "what is your clan good at?"

### Late Game: Mastery as Culture

You've hit 20 people. You have multiple farms. Your territory is secure. You upgrade your Tailor to **Tier 3**.

The building expands into a three-hut compound. Three workers live there now. They never hunt. They never gather. They wake up, work at their craft, eat the food your hunters bring, and go to sleep.

They make **Master cloaks** as baseline. Every single one is +50% better than Common. And occasionally — maybe 1% of the time with perfect materials — they craft a **Legendary cloak** that has special properties (stealth in forests, cold immunity, whatever).

Your clan's warriors are wearing Master gear. You look at your rival's Common equipment and feel superior.

**But you're also a target.** Enemies know you're rich. Raiding your compound means Master-quality loot. Killing your artisans means you lose that advantage.

## The Bigger Picture: Simulating Human History

Here's why we care about getting this right:

**Human craft didn't evolve because of research.** It evolved because:

- **Communities grew** (more people = surplus)
- **Food security improved** (farming, better hunting grounds)
- **Knowledge was passed down** (apprenticeships, teaching)
- **Time was dedicated** (not everyone hunted anymore)
- **Prestige emerged** (showing off your skill became valuable)

That's what we're simulating. When you see your Tier 3 Forge producing Master obsidian spears, you're not seeing "tech unlocked." You're seeing the result of:

- 20 in-game years of clan survival
- Enough food production to sustain non-combatants
- Three generations of babies growing into adults
- Strategic choices (Forge over Tailor over Kiln)
- Materials gathered from across the island
- Raids survived, territory defended

**That spear has a story.** And it lives in your clan's artisans, not in a global "tech tree unlocked" flag.

If those artisans die, you don't have Master spears anymore. You have to rebuild. Train new people. Survive long enough to get back to Tier 3.

**That's Stone Age life.**

---

## Why "Legendary" Is Rare

One more thing about **Legendary** items: they're not a tier. They're **special.**

Even a Tier 3 artisan only has a 1% chance to craft one with perfect materials. They're rare. They're awe-inspiring. They're the flint sword shaped like bronze — the piece that makes other clans stop and say, *"How the hell did they make that?"*

Legendary items might also come from:
- **Hunt drops** — killing a legendary bear gives a pelt that's just... different
- **Quest rewards** — finding an ancient relic or completing a hard challenge
- **Raid loot** — stealing another clan's prized item

They're not part of the progression. They're **moments**. And when you get one, it feels earned.

---

## What This Isn't

Let's be clear on what we're **not** promising:

- **Not a grind.** Tiers aren't "make 1000 cloaks to level up." They're gated by population and food security.
- **Not required.** You can win with Tier 1 buildings if you're aggressive and raid for gear. Tier 3 is prestige, not mandatory.
- **Not promising every craft.** We're showing the system design. Which buildings get three tiers depends on implementation priority.
- **Not instant.** Getting to Tier 3 takes time because it means growing your population, securing food, and gathering upgrade materials.

---

## Closing Thoughts

Stone Age Clans is about **emergent stories from realistic systems.**

A tech tree would flatten that. Everyone follows the same unlock path. Everyone gets the same tools at the same milestones. It's predictable. It's game-like.

**Our tier system lets human craft evolution breathe.**

Your Tier 3 Tailor clan feels different from your neighbor's Tier 3 Forge clan. Your Master cloaks tell a story about the choices you made, the threats you survived, and the artisans you protected.

And when you raid their compound and see three workers tanning leather in Master-quality workshops, you know exactly what's at stake:

**That's not tech. That's culture. And you can take it.**
